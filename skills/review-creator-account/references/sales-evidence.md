# Sales questions and linked funnel evidence

Resolve the platform, account, product, metric definitions, observation periods and attribution
window from the supplied context. Clarify only missing distinctions that affect the comparison.
Public views, likes and caption wording do not measure clicks, inquiries, paid orders or revenue.
If the requested sales metric is unavailable, say that its change cannot be verified; then give
useful public observations and a concrete way to test the proposed explanation. Comparable supplied
revenue can establish a revenue change even without order counts, but not a change in order count,
conversion rate or its cause. Keep each conclusion tied to its own measured outcome.
The headline, opening diagnosis and pattern headings must respect this same boundary. A caveat
later in the answer cannot repair a title that says a content style sells or a CTA caused losses.

For a supplied funnel table, work from its linked cohorts and declared denominators. Label
synthetic scenarios as scenarios, not observed account results. When the same account/product,
metric definitions, observation duration and attribution are comparable, compute each available
transition separately: clicks/views, inquiries/clicks and paid orders/inquiries. Show the arithmetic
and input volumes as well as rates; fewer upstream visits can reduce orders with every transition
rate unchanged. Identify only changes the supplied figures establish, including no rate change,
without inventing their cause.
Stable downstream ratios do not support blaming closing ability when the loss occurs upstream.
If a denominator is zero the rate is undefined; missing values are unknown, not zero.
Still answer the supported transitions when one column is missing.
Calculate every displayed derived rate and change with a local arithmetic tool before writing,
including optional totals. Copy the verified values; check percentage scaling and consistency
between linked transition rates and the overall rate. Keep undefined or unknown results explicit.
Check counts and differences in prose too: the number that did not convert in one period is not
the number of additional losses compared with the other period. State the denominator and unit. When describing non-converters, distinguish the original cohort size from its non-converting subset; do not make that subset the denominator again.
An aggregate difference does not identify particular customers or unpaid orders. Request the
actual inquiry/order records for investigation; do not invent a list of missing transactions.

Different period lengths, cohort ages or attribution windows require alignment before calling
raw totals a decline. Dividing by days alone does not repair different attribution. Explain what
must be matched and what remains descriptive. Even aligned funnel changes identify where a loss
appears, not whether price, customer service, creative or an algorithm caused it. Give testable
hypotheses and the observation that would distinguish them. Recovery after changing several factors at once does not establish which caused the original drop; never turn that test into a definitive if/then diagnosis.
Check observation duration and attribution duration as two separate dimensions before calculating
cross-period changes. If either differs, state each period's supplied duration for both dimensions
in the answer, identify every mismatch, and request matched windows before judging the change.
Do not stop after noticing the first mismatch. While either window is unmatched, do not use equal raw totals to rule out an upstream problem or unequal rates to locate the loss; show those as descriptive values only until both windows are aligned. Unknown durations remain unknown; do not assume
alignment. Accept an explicit supplied assumption of the same measurement and attribution basis for linked cohorts; do not re-label it unknown just because a separate duration in days was not repeated. Clarify only a missing or contradictory comparison basis. Equal declared windows need no extra confirmation. For posts, also check elapsed time
since publication: a recent post's cumulative count cannot rank its quality against an older post.

A supplied narrow funnel question can be answered from that table without fetching public history
as a substitute for private metrics. Do not insert supplied private totals into a public snapshot
or claim the public account report validated them. In a full public review, keep observations and
proposed tests separate from any owner-provided figures and their scope.
When the supplied table answers the requested funnel comparison, finish that comparison directly;
do not append a public-content review unless the user also requested one. Unrelated public
captions cannot resolve a missing private outcome or repair incompatible observation windows.

Before delivery, read the title, first answer and every pattern as standalone claims: does each
say only what its source measures? Remove unsupported sales conclusions there, rather than adding
another disclaimer. Proposed tests should state what to hold comparable and which linked outcome
to record; do not promise growth or sales.
Without measured funnel outcomes, do not locate the problem in converting attention to orders or
label a section as established sales blockers. Wording or offer-clarity concerns remain proposed
tests; mark that uncertainty in the heading and first claim, not just at the end of the paragraph.
