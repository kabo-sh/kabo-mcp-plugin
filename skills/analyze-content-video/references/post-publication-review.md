# Review observed results before attributing them

Apply this gate to initial diagnoses and follow-ups, including results after earlier advice.
Reuse facts already supplied; ask together only for the missing facts needed for this decision.
An earlier assistant's platform assumption or causal claim is not verified evidence.

## Reuse or retrieve available evidence

Reuse supplied records and verified artifacts. When the platform and target are known, use the
existing authorized metadata/account-history tools and workflow to fill publicly available
publication times and comparison data before asking the creator for them. Do not stop a normal
public-data diagnosis simply because the user did not type a baseline. Ask only about what remains
unavailable, ambiguous or private; do not guess the platform to retrieve unrelated evidence.

## Choose the response branch first

For a post comparison, check the actual platform, analytics definition, elapsed time since
publication and a same-age comparison. For account-level source/audience or period comparisons,
check matching populations and observation windows instead; a single post age is not required.
If the relevant basis remains missing after available evidence is checked, this turn is a
clarification: ask one bundled question covering every unresolved item, including the comparison
basis, and end the reply. Do not append a leading diagnosis, an edit prescription, or a claim
about what "most likely" happened. Putting questions after a diagnosis does not satisfy this gate.
Even a tentative statement that a subgroup watched most of the video is an interpretation:
an average duration alone does not identify that subgroup. Do not add a metric interpretation to a clarification question. Earlier assistant language, including the platform it assumed, does not fill missing evidence.
Once the context is aligned, use the hypothesis and testing branch below. This is not a permanent
refusal: supplied definitions and a valid comparison should unlock useful analysis.

## Align the observation

- Identify the actual platform, content format and post. A host default or earlier mention of
  another platform does not establish where the reported numbers came from. Follow the creator's
  explicit correction; if unresolved, ask rather than carry over another platform's terminology.
- Preserve the exact metric label, unit, denominator and observation window. Ask for the relevant
  analytics labels or screenshot when ambiguous. Views, unique reach, impressions, starts and
  engaged views are not interchangeable; skip metrics may measure different events/populations.
- Record publication time and measurement time (or elapsed time), and the comparison post/cohort
  at the same age and format. A small early snapshot alone does not establish poor performance,
  a distribution penalty or failure of the previous recommendation. Do not invent a universal
  waiting period or sample threshold; agree a check time suited to the creator's baseline.
- For advice follow-up, connect the earlier proposal, what actually changed in the published
  version, the observed result and the next test. If the implemented version is unavailable,
  do not assume the recommendation was applied exactly or blame the creator for the result.

## Keep arithmetic separate from meaning

Average watch time divided by video length is a ratio of supplied values, not the share of people
who completed the video or proof that those who stayed watched to the end. Replays, eligibility
rules and the averaging population can affect its meaning. Do not infer a viewer subgroup without
its denominator. Likewise, one minus a skip rate is a watch rate only when the metric explicitly
defines exhaustive complementary outcomes for the same population and window.

Raw comment counts do not establish engagement rate or distribution quality. More comments with
fewer views can describe different audiences, exposure or observation ages; it does not prove
that a hook failed or that a platform withheld distribution. Do not invent algorithm weights.

## Offer useful, testable hypotheses

Once the observation is aligned, separate observed facts, possible explanations and unknowns.
A retention/skip pattern can make an opening worth testing; it cannot by itself identify the
opening as the cause. For each proposed explanation, state what evidence would support or weaken
it and a feasible check. Keep title/caption-based hypotheses separate from unseen video content.

While essential context is missing, end the reply after the bundled clarification question.
Do not append a check plan or diagnosis. Once the missing basis is supplied, offer the test plan
and use the existing calculation/report workflow while preserving its evidence limits.

Prefer one change at a time, a like-for-like comparison at an agreed post age, and a decision
rule for the next iteration. Explain that an observational comparison can remain confounded:
changing one variable improves the experiment but does not by itself prove causation. Retain
successful parts of earlier advice without asserting that they caused the observed outcome.
