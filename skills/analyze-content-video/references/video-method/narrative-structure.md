# Narrative structure

Deeper method for how the video is built from beginning to end: the default card's `structure`,
`proof`, `payoff` and `duration`, and the whole-video trace. Use it when the creator asks about the
story, the script, the flow, why it drags in the middle, or whether the ending works.

## Build a beat map first

Before any judgment, list the beats with their time spans and the ids they rest on. A beat is a
span that does one job:

| Job | What it does for the viewer |
|---|---|
| Setup | Who, where, what is at stake |
| Promise | What the viewer will get by staying |
| Complication | A problem, obstacle, objection or twist that raises the stakes |
| Proof | Something shown that makes the claim believable |
| Turn | The moment the situation or understanding changes |
| Payoff | The promise kept |
| Ask | The requested action |
| Filler | Nothing new: restatement, recap, wind-up, dead air |

Name filler honestly; it is where most edits come from. A beat map from sampled frames and cues is
partial: unseen spans stay unconfirmed.

## Connections between beats

The strongest progressions link beats by contrast or consequence: "this happened, **but** then…",
"this is true, **so**…". A sequence linked by "and then" (another tip, another clip, another
point) gives the viewer no reason to expect the next beat to matter more than the last. Look for:

- **Escalation**: does each beat raise the stakes, add a step toward the result, or deepen the
  reveal? Or are later beats the same size as earlier ones?
- **Open loops**: a question raised early and answered late is what carries a viewer through the
  middle. A loop closed in the first five seconds carries nothing.
- **Order of value** in a list: the strongest item first wins the opening and leaves nothing to
  wait for; the strongest last risks losing viewers before it. Usually: a strong one first, the
  strongest last, and a reason given early to stay for it.

## Structure by genre

| Genre | Typical working shape | Where it usually breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Tutorial | result shown → steps in order → result checked | steps skipped or merged; result never shown again |
| Story, vlog | want → obstacle → attempt → turn → change | no stated want; the turn told, not shown |
| Skit | recognisable setup → escalation → reversal | escalation flat; reversal visible from the start |
| Product | problem → trigger-to-result demonstration → offer | demonstration replaced by claims; offer before value |
| List, tips | promise of N → items with rising value → best last | items of equal weight; the count not kept |
| Commentary | claim → support → implication | support stated, not shown; no implication for the viewer |

Judge the video against its own shape, not another genre's.

## Whole-video checks

- **Repetition**: name the idea that repeats and locate both occurrences, with ids. One recap at the
  end of a long video can be useful; the same benefit line three times is filler.
- **Missing beats**: before saying a beat is missing, check the whole video (frames, cues, viewer
  notes). Distinguish a glimpse from an understandable beat: a result shown for half a second
  while the speaker talks over it is not a demonstration.
- **Promise and payoff**: does the payoff match the opening in kind (a reveal for a reveal, a
  result for a result) and size? The promise-payoff result in the deterministic results anchors the
  promise and lists candidate payoff cues. Name the cue you judge pays it off; do not report a
  distance no script computed.
- **Ask placement**: after the payoff, not before it, and one ask, not several.
- **Ending**: the video ends when the payoff lands, or on a loop back to the opening. A recap,
  a second ask, a trailing still or silence after it are length without value.

## Fixes

- **Reorder**: name the beat to move and where it goes ("move the before/after at 0:34 to right
  after the promise at 0:03").
- **Cut filler**: give the span and what is lost (nothing, or a line worth keeping elsewhere).
- **Add the missing connection**: one line that turns "and then" into "but" or "so"; write it.
- **Split**: a video carrying two promises can become two videos; say which beats go where.
- **Show the turn**: when the change is only told, name the shot that would show it.

## Evidence this needs

- The transcript across the whole video for spoken arguments; frames across the body for visual
  stories; viewer observations for what happens between sampled frames.
- A video with no speech is mapped from frames, on-screen text and viewer notes, and says so.

## Do not claim

- Where viewers dropped off or which beat lost them: no retention curve here.
- That a beat is missing from a span nobody saw.
- That the structure caused the video's performance.
