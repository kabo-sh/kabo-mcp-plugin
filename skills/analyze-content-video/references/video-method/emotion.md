# Emotion

Deeper method for how a video makes the viewer feel and when: its emotional arc, where it turns,
what carries each feeling, and how to keep or transfer it. Use it when the creator asks about mood,
feeling, tension, why it is moving or funny, or wants new openings or videos "with this video's
emotion". There is no emotion dimension: file statements under the dimension of the carrier
(`delivery` for face and voice, `audio` for music, `pacing` for timing, `spoken_hook` or
`structure` for words and order, `visual_hook` or `framing` for the picture).

## Name the feeling precisely

"Emotional" is not a reading. Name the intended feeling with a word a viewer would use, and keep
close ones apart:

| Family | Distinguish |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | intrigue (what is it?), suspense (what happens next?), disbelief (is that real?) |
| Amusement | recognition humour (that's so me), absurdity, schadenfreude, cuteness |
| Tension | dread, urgency, embarrassment on someone's behalf |
| Warmth | nostalgia, tenderness, belonging, pride in someone |
| Aspiration | envy, awe, "I could do that" |
| Indignation | unfairness, outrage, being lied to |
| Release | relief, satisfaction (a thing done perfectly), catharsis |

Each is an inference about the intended effect, labelled as such. What viewers actually felt is not
in the evidence: no comments, survey or retention here.

## Map the arc

List the emotional beats with time spans, and for each: the intended feeling, its intensity (low,
rising, peak, falling) relative to the rest of the video, and the carriers with their ids.

**Carriers** — what produces the feeling at that moment:

- **Face**: expression and change of expression in frames (describe what is visible: "eyes closed,
  mouth open" — not inner states).
- **Voice**: pace, pitch, volume, pauses; from viewer notes and loudness measures.
- **Music and sound**: presence, character, energy, a drop to silence; from heard notes and
  measures (silence spans, loudness changes). Never from a track's title.
- **Pacing**: cut rate and shot length (`media.probed.shots`); slowing before a reveal, speeding
  through a montage.
- **Words**: what is said and written; the line that names the feeling or reverses it.
- **Picture**: framing (a push to a close-up), light, colour, what is revealed.

## Where it turns

The turn is where the feeling changes: calm to shock, tension to release, comedy to sincerity. Find
it and check:

- **Is it signalled by more than one carrier?** A turn that lands on face, sound and cut together
  reads; a turn carried by one line alone while the music keeps going can be missed.
- **Is there room before it?** A held beat, a pause, a drop in sound gives the turn contrast. A
  turn crammed between two fast cuts loses it.
- **Is it earned?** The setup must build the opposite feeling first; release needs tension before
  it.
- **Does the video stay at the peak too long, or leave it too fast?** After the peak, one beat to
  land, then the ask or the end.

## Common failures

- **One intensity throughout**: no contrast, so no turn.
- **Carriers disagree**: an upbeat track under a sad story, a smiling delivery of bad news (unless
  it is the joke).
- **The emotional peak after the ask**, or cut off by an end card.
- **Opening feeling unrelated to the body**: shock in the hook, calm explainer after it; the viewer
  who stopped for the shock leaves.
- **Told, not shown**: "I was so nervous" over a neutral face and steady music.

## Fixes

- Move the peak earlier or protect it: "end at 0:41 on her reaction; cut the end card".
- Give the turn room: "hold the frame before the reveal at 0:18 and drop the music to silence for
  that beat".
- Align carriers: "lower the track under the confession at 0:25 so the voice carries it".
- Show the feeling: a close-up of the reaction instead of a line describing it.

## Transferring an emotion to a new opening or video

When the creator wants openings or videos with this video's emotion, transfer the mechanism, never
the wording or the footage:

1. **The recipe**: the feeling, the trigger (what causes it: a reveal, a contrast, a recognisable
   situation, a vulnerable admission), the carriers that deliver it, and the timing (how fast it
   arrives, what precedes it).
2. **The creator's material**: which trigger they can produce from their own topic, face, voice and
   footage. A trigger that needs material they do not have is a preparation task.
3. **Each new opening**: the first frame, the first sound and the first words that produce the same
   feeling in the first seconds, written as one decision. On someone else's video, write new lines;
   never reuse theirs.
4. **Say what may not transfer**: a feeling that depends on that creator's known persona or long
   story arc may not land for a new audience.

## Evidence this needs

- Frames for faces and pictures; transcript for words; viewer observations for voice, music and
  what moves between frames; measures for timing, loudness and silence.
- A reading from one carrier alone is weaker; say which carriers were available.

## Do not claim

- What viewers felt or that the emotion drove shares or comments.
- A person's inner state; describe the visible expression and the heard voice.
- Lyrics, a song's identity by ear, or that the music's peak lands on a cut.
