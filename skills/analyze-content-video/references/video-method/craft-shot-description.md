# Shot description for a craft teardown

The base method when the creator asks how a video was made. It decides how shots are described,
how the answer is organised and what each claim stands on. The craft teardown workflow and what
that mode writes are in `references/craft-teardown.md`.

## Look before describing

Frames arrive as references; a reference is not a picture. Open the frames before describing any
shot: framing, shot size, movement, type, colour and where the eye goes all live in the picture.
Read only the words and you have written what the video said, not how it was made.

Use the measure for shot boundaries (`media.probed.shots`: cut times and durations) so each shot
has a start and an end. Without it, bracket each visible change between the two frames around it.

## Five aspects per shot

Describe each shot that matters on the same five aspects, in this order. Mark an aspect "not
visible" rather than skipping it.

1. **Subject** — what is in the shot: who or what, and what the viewer is meant to look at.
2. **Subject motion** — what the subject does: walks into frame, turns to the lens, pours, holds up
   the product, stays still.
3. **Setting** — where: the location, the background, props, time of day when visible.
4. **Framing** — shot size and angle (vocabulary below), and where the subject sits in a vertical
   or horizontal frame.
5. **Camera** — what the camera does: locked off, handheld, a pan, a push-in. Movement needs at
   least two frames inside the same shot (or a viewer note covering it); never infer it from one
   frame.

Then the edit layer on top, where present: captions and text, overlays, inserts, split screen,
speed changes, the transition into the next shot.

## Use the precise names

- **Shot size**: extreme wide, wide, full (head to toe), medium wide (knees up), medium (waist up),
  medium close-up (chest up), close-up (face), extreme close-up (a detail), insert (an object or a
  hand doing something).
- **Angle**: eye level, high angle, low angle, top-down (overhead), Dutch tilt, point of view, over
  the shoulder.
- **Camera movement**: static, handheld, pan (turns left/right), tilt (turns up/down), push-in /
  pull-out (camera moves toward or away), tracking (moves alongside), orbit (circles the subject),
  crane or jib (rises or falls). A zoom changes magnification without the background shifting
  against the foreground; a push-in shifts it. Tell them apart from two frames, or say which is
  unclear.
- **Cuts and transitions**: hard cut, jump cut (same subject, slight jump in time), match cut
  (shape or motion continues across the cut), cut on action, whip (fast pan blur joining two
  shots), mask or object wipe (something passes the lens and reveals the next shot), speed ramp,
  dissolve. Audio leading or trailing a cut can be heard, not seen.

Say "a medium close-up with a slow push-in", not "a nice shot".

## Organise the answer around the question

Walking the timeline from second zero answers no craft question. Shape the answer by what was
asked:

- **"What can I reuse?"** A ranked list of reusable techniques, each with where it appears and why
  it works. Say what you ranked by.
- **"How do I get this look?"** The passes that produce it, in order: shooting (light, framing,
  movement), editing (cuts, speed), finishing (colour, text, sound).
- **"How is it paced?"** The measurements first (shot count, shot lengths by section, holds), then
  the judgment.
- **"How was this transition / effect done?"** The shots on both sides, the five aspects for each,
  then the steps.

**Rank when ranking is possible**, and say why: "these three matter most because they repeat in
every scene" beats seven items of equal weight.

**When the reference does not match the creator's situation, say so and redirect.** A 20-second ad
with a crew and a three-minute solo vlog do not share time sinks. Saying so early is the most
useful thing a teardown can do.

## Which of this can one person do

Under almost every craft question sits "can I do this alone?". Answer it for each technique:

- **Alone, with a phone**: say so and how.
- **Alone, if…**: name the condition (a tripod, a second take for each angle, a free editor
  feature, an hour of practice). "You can, if" with no "if" is just "yes".
- **Needs help or gear**: say which part and whether a simpler version gets most of the effect.

Name editor features by what they do or by the name the editor uses for them; do not invent click
paths or menu locations, which move between versions.

## Two levels of claim

Keep them distinguishable in the prose:

- **Read off this video**: cite the frame, cue or measure. "The captions sit in the upper third"
  belongs here only if you looked at the frame.
- **General practice**: not from this video. Give settings and numbers when they help (an export
  bitrate, a loudness target, a frame rate), but visibly as general practice: "a common starting
  point is…", never stated flat like a measurement.

## Do not claim

- How the video performed or how a technique will perform for the creator: a teardown holds no
  metrics or baseline.
- The camera, lens, gear, app or preset used, unless shown on screen or stated in the video.
- Camera movement from a single frame, or cut timing without the measure.
- A song or artist identified by ear (a title comes only from metadata, on-screen text or speech),
  lyrics quoted or paraphrased, or cuts said to land on the beat without a beat measurement.
- Someone else's wording as a line to reuse: borrow the technique, not the words.
