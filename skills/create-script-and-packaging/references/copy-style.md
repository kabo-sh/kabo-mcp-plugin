# Copy style: samples, style requests and rejections

Read when the creator names a style ("more premium", "punchier", "less salesy"), supplies copy to
imitate, or rejects earlier copy without saying exactly what to change ("too generic", "boring",
"too long", "not good"). The aim is to land the style in one or two turns, not ten.

## Carry the style forward

Before writing, collect from the whole thread, not only the last message: the creator's language,
any sample or earlier copy they liked, explicit tone words, things they said to avoid, and
directions they already rejected. Put them in `creative_direction`: `tone` for the target in a
few words, `voice_rules` for concrete rules ("max 8 words per line", "no exclamation marks",
"second person"), `banned_phrases` for wording they rejected or asked to drop. A rejected
direction stays banned for the rest of the thread unless the creator brings it back.

## A sample to imitate

When the creator pastes copy they like, the sample is the specification. Match its length (word
count per line and overall), structure (line breaks, list or prose, where the hook sits), tone,
vocabulary level and punctuation habits. In one short line before the copy, name the two or three
traits you are matching, so the creator can correct the reading instead of the whole draft. Do not
reuse its sentences or claims; imitate form, not content.

## A style word without a sample

When the request is for copy (hooks, captions, titles, a short script) and the style word could be
read in clearly different ways, give two or three short directions that differ on the dimension the
word is about, each as one sample line plus a two-to-four-word label, and ask which to develop.
Keep it to a few lines; do not write two or three full drafts. When the request is unambiguous or
the creator asked for a finished piece without discussion, write it in one stated direction and
offer the alternative in one line.

## A rejection without a reason

Rejection words map to a dimension:

| What they say | Usually means | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| too generic, could be anyone | specificity | concrete details from their topic, product or audience; fewer abstractions |
| boring, flat | hook and energy | stronger opening tension, contrast or a surprising detail; shorter lines |
| too long | length | cut at least a third; one idea per line |
| not premium, cheap, salesy | tone and vocabulary | fewer superlatives and calls to buy, calmer rhythm, precise nouns |
| not me, not my voice | voice | follow their own earlier posts or sample; ask for one if none exists |

If the word does not identify a dimension and no earlier message does, ask one short question with
the likely options ("shorter, more specific, or a different tone?") instead of producing another
round in the same style. Each new version must change the identified dimension visibly, not
reword the previous one. Say in one line what you changed.

## Defaults

Write short-form copy concise by default: short lines, no filler, one call to action. Reply and
write in the creator's language; copy for a stated audience language follows that language.
